\subsection{The Foothold Identity}
Every host was assessed from the same account. Its lack of privilege is what makes the results
significant --- nothing below required any special group or sudo right.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{0.92\linewidth}{@{}l Y@{}}
\toprule
\rowcolor{TableHead}
\textcolor{white}{\bfseries Attribute} & \textcolor{white}{\bfseries Value (identical on all hosts)} \\
\midrule
User              & \fillme{username} \\
\rowcolor{TableAlt}
Privileged groups & \fillme{None (no sudo, wheel, admin, docker, lxd, disk, adm, shadow, staff)} \\
\texttt{sudo -l}  & \fillme{No output on any host -- no cached credential, no sudoers entry} \\
\rowcolor{TableAlt}
Container?        & \fillme{No -- direct host access on every machine} \\
\bottomrule

\end{center}

\begin{infobox}{Why one identity across the whole fleet matters}
\small
\fillme{Explain the shared-identity / shared-storage implication: a credential or key harvested on
one host is likely to work on the others, so the fleet behaves as one trust domain rather than
independently defended machines.}
\end{infobox}
